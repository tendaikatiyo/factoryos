How a flexible packaging plant works

From plastic pellets to printed rolls, laminates and finished bags: the machines, the materials and the route every order follows at Rawplast Industries.

Msasa · head office and main plant Blown film extruders EX01–EX18 · rewinder · stack printers PR01–PR04 · laminator · slitter · bag makers · core machine · two recyclers · raw material stores
Southerton · 12 km away, since 2022 Machinery acquired from City Plastics. Central impression printer PR05 and bag makers. Film arrives from Msasa as WIP; printed rolls that need slitting return to Msasa.
1

The route of an order

Make-to-order. Every run starts with a customer order. The Order Confirmation is the contract.

The Works Order is the factory instruction. It ticks the stops this job needs. It is never altered: a change gets a new works order.

Output is counted in kg and units, within ±10% of the order. The plant runs two shifts.

2

Raw materials

POLYETHYLENE PELLETS · melted in the extruders

LDPE · low density. Soft, clear and stretchy. Shrink film, liners, sheeting and soft bags.

LLDPE · linear low density. Tough and puncture resistant. Blended in for strength and cling; key to pallet wrap.

HDPE · high density. Stiff, crisp and strong at thin gauges. Carrier bags and crinkly film.

Recycled pellets · made in-house from factory scrap. Used for black sheeting and in blends.

Additives · small doses in the mix: colour masterbatch, slip (film slides), antiblock (layers part easily), UV (outdoor life), antistatic.

FILMS · arrive ready-made on rolls

BOPP · biaxially oriented polypropylene. Clear, glossy, stiff and easy to print. Bought in the supplier's standard widths, so slitting to size leaves offcuts.

MBOPP · BOPP with a thin metal coating. Blocks light, moisture and air for snacks and dry foods.

CONSUMABLES

Inks, solvents, adhesive · solvent inks for flexo, solvent for thinning and wash-up, adhesive to bond laminates. Kept in a controlled ink store.

Paper cores · tubes every roll is wound on. Made on site by the core machine.

Formulation: the works order sets the recipe as Base / LLDPE / Additive. Stores issue it against a material requisition.

3

Blown film extrusion

EX01–EX18
Msasa

How it works

Pellets drop from the hopper into a heated barrel. A turning screw melts the plastic and pushes it up through a ring-shaped die as a thin tube. Air blown into the tube inflates it into a tall bubble while the air ring cools it from outside. At the top, nip rollers flatten the bubble into layflat tubing and the film is wound onto a roll. Cutting the edges gives sheeting.

What the operator sets

Temperatures in the barrel, adaptor and die, screw RPM, die size and niproller speed. A faster nip pulls the film thinner, which lowers the gauge.

Corona treatment

Polyethylene repels ink. An electric discharge raises the film surface energy so ink and adhesive grip. UT untreated · T1 one side · T2 both sides.

At Rawplast

Each extruder has one operator. The operator mixes the formulation in a half drum and loads the hopper by bucket. Rolls are wound to a target weight and labelled with weight and customer; the label follows the roll through every stage. Several extruders can share one works order. A rewinder turns big pallet wrap rolls into smaller ones.

Types of extruder

Blown film · the EX fleet

Tube inflated into a bubble. Strong in both directions. Monolayer at Rawplast.

Cast film

Flat die onto a chilled roller. Very clear film with an even gauge; common for stretch wrap.

Co-extrusion

Two to seven extruders feed one die to build layered film, each layer with a job: seal, barrier or print.

Flexographic printing

PR01–PR04 Msasa
PR05 Southerton

How it works

Each station prints one colour, and a job uses one station per colour. Ink flows from the pan to the anilox roller, a doctor blade wipes off the excess, and the anilox inks only the raised parts of the plate. The plate presses that ink onto the film as it passes the impression cylinder. Dryers set the ink before the next colour.

At Rawplast

A printer runs with up to three operators. They mount plates, set each station and mix inks to match the QA-approved swatch kept at the machine. A test run is checked, and the order runs once QA approves. The printing report records the colours, the anilox on each station and the ink codes for every run.

Repeat and register

The size of the plate cylinder sets the repeat, the length of one round of the design. Small marks printed beside the design let the crew line each colour up with the last, which is called keeping register.

Stack printer

Stations stand in towers on both sides of a frame and the film travels between them. Each station is easy to reach, so changeovers are quick and short runs pay. Keeping colours in register takes care, because the film can stretch between towers.

Central impression

Every station prints against one big drum, and the film rides on that drum through all colours. Register stays tight at high speed. PR05 is German-built with print quality compared to gravure. It pays from about 1 tonne per run; each wash-up uses 50–100 litres of solvent.

Gravure is the other major print process in the industry. The image is engraved as tiny cells into a metal cylinder that is flooded with ink and wiped. It gives superb quality and suits very long runs, since each cylinder is costly to make. Rawplast's fleet is all flexo.

Laminating and slitting

Lamination bonds two films with adhesive so the pack gets both sets of properties: BOPP for print and gloss, MBOPP as a barrier to light, moisture and air. The print usually sits on the inside face of the top film, sealed safely between the layers. Printing operators run the laminator, and laminated rolls usually go on to slitting for autopack.

The slitter unwinds a wide roll, blades cut it into narrower rolls and trim the edges, and each strip rewinds onto its own core. A job can leave the factory straight from slitting. Southerton's printed rolls come back to Msasa for slitting.

6

Bag making

Msasa and Southerton

Bottom seal

Made from tubing. One seal runs across the bottom; the sides are the tube's own folds. Strong for heavy loads, liners and refuse bags.

Side seal

Made from folded film. Seals run down both sides and the bottom is the fold. Ideal for printed bags, since the print runs edge to edge.

Each bag maker has one operator. A heated seal bar presses the layers until they fuse, and a knife cuts each bag free. Plain rolls make plain bags and printed rolls make branded bags. Output is counted in units and weighed in kg. Options set on the works order:

Gusset · tucked folds that let the bag expand

Flap / flip · extra lip that folds over the mouth

Punch · die-cut handle holes

Hem · folded, reinforced edge

Handles · loops or patches for carrying

Perforation · tear line between bags

Emboss · texture pressed into the film

C-fold · sheet folded for dispensing

Sleeves · open-ended tubes

7

Support machines

Two-stage recyclers

Operators feed all the factory's film scrap into the recyclers. Stage one grinds and melts it; stage two cools the strands and chops them into pellets. The pellets are booked back into raw material stores and used for black sheeting and blends.

Power limits: the two recyclers cannot run together, and on generator power a recycler cannot run alongside the printers.

Core machine

Winds glued paper strips into tubes and cuts them to length. Cores are sold, used for the plant's own rolls and for handheld pallet wrap. The machine sits in the bag making wing at Msasa, next to the slitter.
